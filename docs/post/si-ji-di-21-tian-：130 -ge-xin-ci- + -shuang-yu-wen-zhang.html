双语文章 · A First-Aid Training for Volunteers / 一次志愿者急救培训

读法: 先读英文段落,遇到加粗的今日新词停一下回想意思,再看下面的中文。

Last month, the Red Cross advertised a free first-aid training for volunteers in our community. A big red banner hung at the entrance of the subway station: "Learn to save a life in one weekend." My classmate Wang Lei and I signed up at once. We both felt that the course would be beneficial not only to others but also to ourselves.

上个月,红十字会发布广告,为我们社区的志愿者提供免费急救培训。地铁站入口处挂着一条红色大横幅:"一个周末,学会挽救生命。"我和同学王磊立刻报了名。我们都觉得这门课程不仅对别人有益,对我们自己也有好处。

The training was held in a community center on Park Avenue. Our trainer, Dr. Chen, had worked in a hospital emergency room for twenty years. She was charming and funny, but very systematic in her teaching. First she gave each of us a handbook that she had compiled herself. Then she clarified the goal of the course: "In an urgent situation, you are not a doctor. Your job is to keep the person alive until help arrives."

培训在公园大街上的一个社区中心举行。我们的培训老师陈医生在医院急诊室工作了二十年。她很有魅力,说话风趣,教学却非常有条理。她先发给我们每人一本她亲自编写的手册,然后阐明了课程的目标:"在紧急情况下,你们不是医生。你们的任务是让伤者活着,直到救援到来。"

The first lesson was about bleeding. Dr. Chen explained that a deep cut can bleed heavily, and that losing too much blood within a short duration can be deadly. She showed us how to press on the wound with a clean cloth and how to tie a knot in a bandage so that it would not come loose. "Don't neglect small wounds either," she said. "A dirty cut can easily become infected." She added that crude home remedies, such as putting ash or vinegar on a wound, are unnecessary and can even be harmful.

第一课讲的是出血。陈医生解释说,很深的伤口可能会大量流血,在很短的时间内失血过多可能致命。她示范了怎样用干净的布按压伤口,以及怎样给绷带打一个不会松开的结。"小伤口也不要忽视,"她说,"不干净的伤口很容易感染。"她还说,往伤口上抹灰或醋之类粗陋的土方法是不必要的,甚至可能有害。

Next we learned what to do when someone chokes on food. Wang Lei volunteered to be the patient. He pretended that a pea was stuck in his throat and made a funny face. Everyone laughed, but Dr. Chen said, "This is serious. Every year, many people, especially old people and small children, die this way." She stood behind him, placed her hands above his waist and showed us the right movements. When it was my turn, I felt awkward and my hands began to tremble. "Be cautious, but don't be afraid," she told me.

接着我们学习有人被食物噎住时该怎么办。王磊自告奋勇当"病人"。他假装一颗豌豆卡在了喉咙里,还做了个滑稽的鬼脸。大家都笑了,但陈医生说:"这是很严肃的事。每年都有很多人,特别是老人和小孩,因此丧命。"她站到他身后,双手放在他腰部上方,给我们演示正确的动作。轮到我时,我觉得很笨拙,双手开始发抖。"要谨慎,但别害怕,"她对我说。

The hardest part was CPR. We practised on a plastic model lying on a rug. Dr. Chen told us to stay alert and first check whether the person was breathing. Then we had to press hard on the chest about a hundred times a minute, taking turns with a partner in a steady rotation so that no one got too tired. After ten minutes my arms felt heavy, and I almost gave up in despair. But she said, "The probability of survival drops quickly with every minute. Don't halt until the ambulance arrives."
