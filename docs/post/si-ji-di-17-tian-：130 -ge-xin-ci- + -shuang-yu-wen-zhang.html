双语文章 · My First Internship in a Big City / 我在大城市的第一次实习

读法: 先读英文段落,遇到加粗的今日新词停一下回想意思,再看下面的中文。

Last summer, I got an internship at a technology company in Shanghai. On the day of my departure, my mother gave me an envelope with some money and a short letter. "Be generous with others and precise in your work," it said. I felt both excited and anxious. I had never lived alone before, and the big city seemed like a strange new realm.

去年夏天,我在上海一家科技公司得到了一份实习。出发那天,妈妈给了我一个装着一些钱和一封短信的信封。信上写着:"待人要大方,做事要精确。"我既兴奋又焦虑。我以前从没一个人生活过,这座大城市就像一个陌生的新领域。

When I arrived at my destination, a shuttle bus took me from the station to the company. The reception area was bright and modern, with a large metal statue in the middle. A friendly clerk checked my name, gave me a card, and pointed to the elevator. "Your team is on the twelfth floor," she said with a mild smile. The doors opened automatically as I walked in.

到达目的地后,一辆班车把我从车站接到了公司。前台区域明亮又现代,中间立着一座大型金属雕像。一位友善的职员核对了我的名字,给了我一张卡,然后指了指电梯。"你的团队在十二楼,"她带着温和的微笑说。我走进去时,门自动打开了。

My manager, Mr. Zhao, was a tall, handsome man in a casual shirt. He explained that our team was building artificial intelligence software for automobiles. "Our cars can scan the road and stop automatically when something is in front of them," he said. It sounded promising, but also very abstract to me. I had studied the theoretical parts in class, but I had never worked on real hardware.

我的经理赵先生是个高个子、英俊的男人,穿着一件休闲衬衫。他解释说,我们团队在为汽车开发人工智能软件。"我们的汽车能扫描路面,前方有东西时会自动停下来,"他说。这听起来很有前途,但对我来说也非常抽象。我在课堂上学过理论部分,却从没接触过真正的硬件。

The first two weeks were hard. I made silly mistakes in my code, and once I had to cancel an important test because I had connected the wrong circuit. I felt like a fool. In the evenings I went back to my small room, ate noodles under a dim lamp, and felt lonely. Sometimes I hesitated to ask questions because I was reluctant to look stupid.

头两周很难熬。我在代码里犯了一些愚蠢的错误,有一次因为接错了电路,不得不取消一次重要的测试。我觉得自己像个傻瓜。晚上我回到小房间,在昏暗的台灯下吃面条,感到很孤独。有时我犹豫着不敢提问,因为我不愿意显得很笨。

Things changed when Mr. Zhao gave me some honest feedback. "Don't be afraid to make mistakes," he said. "Everyone here stumbled at the beginning. What matters is your determination to learn." He asked an elder colleague, Ms. Wu, to give me guidance. She was patient and outstanding at her job. With her help, I learned to repair small problems myself and to cooperate with the team.
