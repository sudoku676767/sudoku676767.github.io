双语文章 · What Skills Will You Need in the Future? / 未来你需要什么技能?

读法: 先读英文段落,遇到加粗的今日新词停一下回想意思,再看下面的中文。

Last week, our campus magazine held an interview with Professor Zhang, an expert in education and management. Over the past twenty years, she has studied how young people prepare for work. Her latest theory is simple but tough to put into practice: knowledge alone is no longer enough; students also need the right skills.

上周,我们的校园杂志对张教授进行了一次采访。她是教育和管理方面的专家,过去二十年一直在研究年轻人如何为工作做准备。她最新的理论简单,但很难付诸实践:光有知识已经不够了,学生还需要合适的技能。

"Many students imagine that a good degree will establish their future," she said. "That was true for my generation, but the reality is different now. The job market changes throughout a person's life. You must be able to learn new things suddenly, and apply them quickly."

"很多学生以为一个好学位就能奠定他们的未来,"她说。"对我们这一代人来说是这样,但现在的现实不同了。就业市场在一个人的一生中(throughout 始终)不断变化。你必须能够突然学会新东西,并迅速运用。"

According to her analysis, there are three main skills that matter most. The first is critical thinking: the ability to compare ideas, ask questions and find the truth. The second is communication. "Whether you are a lawyer, a writer or a coach, you need to address people clearly and respond to their concerns," she explained. The third is the ability to deal with challenges and fear of failure.

根据她的分析,最重要的有三种主要技能。第一是批判性思维:比较各种观点、提出问题并找到真相的能力。第二是沟通。"无论你是律师、作家还是教练,你都需要清楚地向人们讲话(address),并回应他们的关切,"她解释说。第三是应对挑战和克服对失败的恐惧的能力。

To prove her point, she described a specific study. Her team followed 2,000 adults for ten years. The ones who succeeded did not always have the highest grades. Rather, they tended to share a particular pattern: they could manage their time, work in a unit with others, and improve themselves after mistakes. "Grades measure what you know today," she said. "Skills reveal what you can do tomorrow."

为了证明自己的观点,她介绍了一项具体研究。她的团队跟踪了 2000 名成年人十年。那些成功的人并不总是成绩最高的。相反,他们往往有一种特定的模式:能管理自己的时间,能在一个团体(unit)里和别人合作,能在犯错后提升自己。"成绩衡量的是你今天知道什么,"她说。"技能揭示的是你明天能做什么。"

I asked her what students should do inside and outside the classroom. Her answer was clearly practical. "Join a club, perform in a play, take a part-time job, or design a small project," she said. "Each item on that list teaches you something a book cannot. Indeed, some of the most useful lessons come from various small experiences that seem unimportant at the time."

我问她学生在课堂内外应该做些什么。她的回答显然很实际。"参加社团、在话剧中表演、做份兼职,或者设计一个小项目,"她说。"清单上的每一项都能教会你书本教不了的东西。的确,一些最有用的经验来自各种当时看起来不重要的小经历。"

She also warned students to avoid a common mistake. "Some young people dream of a huge salary and choose a job only for money. That is not a good strategy. You should notice what you are good at, and think about your purpose. A career should reflect who you are." She added that an entire generation of graduates now exists in a world where jobs come and go. Companies hang on to good employees, but they also remove jobs that machines can do.

她还提醒学生避免一个常见错误。"有些年轻人梦想拿到巨额薪水,只为了钱选择工作。这不是好的策略。你应该留意自己擅长什么,并思考自己的目标(purpose)。职业应该反映你是谁。"她补充说,如今整整一代毕业生生活(exist 存在)在一个工作来来去去的世界里。公司会留住(hang on to)好员工,但也会取消机器能做的工作。

At the end of the discussion, I asked her about her own memory of being a student. She laughed. "I was not a popular student. I spent most of my time in the library. But one teacher saw my potential and gave me a chance to lead a conference. That stage of my life changed my direction completely. Since then, my style of teaching has been to give every student a similar chance."

在讨论的最后,我问她作为学生时的记忆。她笑了。"我那时不是一个受欢迎的学生,大部分时间都待在图书馆。但一位老师看到了我的潜力,给了我主持一个会议的机会。人生的那个阶段彻底改变了我的方向。从那以后,我的教学风格就是给每个学生类似的机会。"

Her final message was onto something important. "Do not wait for the perfect moment," she said. "Discover what you love, maintain your curiosity, and treat every failure as a lesson. The impact you have on the world will affect far more people than you imagine."

她最后的寄语很有道理(be onto something 发现了重要的东西)。"不要等待完美时刻,"她说。"发现你热爱的东西,保持好奇心,把每一次失败都当作一堂课。你对世界的影响所影响到的人,会比你想象的多得多。"
